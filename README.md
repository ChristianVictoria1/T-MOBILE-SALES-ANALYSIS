# T-Mobile Retail Sales Analysis

## Overview
This project is based on real in-store retail interactions that I observed and recorded to identify patterns in customer engagement, sales conversion, customer intent, and accessory attachment.

Using SQL and Power BI, I transformed observations from real retail interactions into measurable insights to better understand customer behavior and identify opportunities to improve store performance.

## Business Problem

Retail stores interact with customers who have different needs, purchasing intentions, and levels of engagement. Understanding how these factors relate to sales performance can help identify opportunities to improve customer experiences and increase conversions.

The objective of this project was to answer three primary questions:

- How does customer intent influence sales conversion?

- How does customer engagement relate to purchasing outcomes?

- Do certain work shifts demonstrate stronger sales conversion performance?

---

## Tools Used

- SQL: Data querying, aggregation, and performance analysis.

- Power BI: Interactive dashboards and data visualization.

- Excel / CSV: Data organization and preparation.

---

## Key Insights
- High-engagement customer interactions had a 58.97% conversion rate, compared to only 15.38% for low-engagement interactions.

- Customers who entered the store just browsing generated the highest revenue, showing that proactive engagement can create sales opportunities.

- The 6:00PM – 8:00PM shift had the highest conversion rate, suggesting evening customers may have stronger purchase intent.

---

## Recommendations
- Focus on increasing customer engagement during every interaction.

- Develop Browsing-to-Purchase Strategies: Focus on product education, demonstrations, and personalized recommendations for customers who initially enter the store to browse.

- Schedule strong sales associates during peak conversion periods to maximize revenue.
